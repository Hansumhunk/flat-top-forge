How the calculator thinks

This is a planning heuristic, not a manufacturer specification. It gives extra space for larger groups, multi-component meals, and cooks who want fewer batches. It separately recommends a minimum range of independent heat controls based on how many temperature jobs you want to run at once. Portability pulls the size recommendation smaller when the other answers allow it.

What the size ranges mean

RangeUsually fitsTradeoff
17–22 inch class1–3 people, camping, tailgating, simple mealsEasy to move, less room for multi-zone cooking
28 inch class2–5 people, ordinary family meals, moderate batchingGood middle ground between space and footprint
30–32 inch class4–7 people, mixed meals, more simultaneous cookingMore workspace with less portability
36 inch and larger5+ people, entertaining, full breakfasts, hibachi-style cooksMaximum room, largest storage and fuel footprint

What the heat-zone ranges mean

ControlsUseful patternTypical fit
1 independent zoneOne general surface temperatureSimple meals and compact electric griddles
2 independent zonesHot + medium/coolMost ordinary family meals
3 independent zonesSear + cook + finish/holdFull breakfasts, fajitas, rice, protein + multiple sides
4 independent zonesSeveral active temperatures plus holding spaceLarge breakfasts, hibachi, parties, high-volume multi-item cooking

Burner count or element count does not guarantee perfectly isolated surface temperatures. Plate material, burner geometry, wind, preheat, and food load all affect real heat distribution.

Use both results together. A large griddle with too few independent controls may not fit your workflow, while a smaller griddle with plenty of controls can still run out of physical space. Compare size and zone count as separate requirements.
