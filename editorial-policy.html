Editorial & research policy

What we count as evidence, what we label as opinion, and how we keep affiliate money from quietly dressing up as research.

Primary sources first

For dimensions, specifications, model compatibility, warranty terms, and stated features, we prioritize manufacturer manuals, specification sheets, official product pages, and other primary documentation.

Hands-on testing is labeled

We do not imply that a product was personally tested when it was not. If a recommendation is based on published specifications, documentation, pricing, or customer feedback, that basis should be clear. If something was cooked on, measured, or tested directly, that should be stated plainly.

Customer feedback is supporting evidence

Owner reviews and community discussions can reveal recurring issues or practical details, but they are not treated as controlled testing. Anecdotes help identify questions worth investigating; they do not magically become specifications because enough people repeated them online.

Affiliate relationships do not purchase conclusions

Some pages may earn commissions through affiliate links. Commercial relationships do not determine whether a product is recommended, and paid placement or sponsorship should be disclosed when it exists.

Corrections and updates

Griddle models, prices, accessories, and product specifications change. Pages should be updated when better information becomes available, and material corrections should favor the newest reliable source over preserving an older conclusion for appearances.
