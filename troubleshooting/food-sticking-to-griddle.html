Troubleshooting / Cooking

Why is food sticking to my griddle?

Sticking is usually a combination of surface condition, heat, moisture, oil, and timing. The spatula is often innocent, despite being blamed at the scene.

Most common causes
Surface

Weak or damaged seasoning

Bare patches, rust, flaking buildup, or a poorly seasoned surface can make release less predictable.

Heat

Wrong temperature

Too cool can prevent browning and release; too hot can burn delicate foods before they naturally let go.

Food prep

Too much moisture

Wet meat, vegetables, or thawed food cools the surface and creates steam before browning.

Technique

Moving food too early

Many proteins release more easily after a crust has formed. Constant poking can turn a normal cook into a scraping contest.

A better sequence

  1. Start with a clean, sound cooking surface.
  2. Preheat before loading food.
  3. Dry ingredients when surface moisture is unnecessary.
  4. Use an appropriate amount of cooking fat for the food and surface.
  5. Give the food time to brown before trying to move it.
  6. If sticking persists across many foods, inspect the seasoning rather than compensating with increasingly heroic quantities of oil.

Different foods behave differently

Eggs and pancakes generally prefer gentler heat and a well-lubricated surface. Smash burgers need much higher heat because the goal is aggressive browning. Chicken, vegetables, fish, and sugary marinades each respond differently, which is why one universal burner setting is mostly wishful thinking.
