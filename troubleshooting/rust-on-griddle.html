How to deal with rust on a griddle

Rust means moisture reached exposed steel. The fix is not mystical: remove the oxidation, get back to a clean stable surface, dry it, then rebuild protection.

First, judge the severity

Light orange surface rust is different from deep pitting, heavy scale, or structural damage. The more severe the corrosion, the less sensible it is to attack the cooktop blindly with whatever power tool happens to be nearby.

For light surface rust

  1. Follow the manufacturer’s restoration instructions for the specific cooktop.
  2. Remove loose rust and failed seasoning with an approved scraper, griddle stone, pad, or other recommended method.
  3. Wipe away all loosened material.
  4. Dry the cooktop completely.
  5. Reseason the cleaned area with multiple very thin oil layers.

For heavy rust or pitting

If corrosion is deep, widespread, flaking, or has significantly damaged the cooking surface, stop treating it like a cosmetic stain. Check the manufacturer’s service guidance and determine whether restoration or replacement makes more sense.

Why rust comes back

Common causes include storing the griddle wet, damaged or incomplete seasoning, condensation under a cover, long periods without use, or weather exposure. A cover helps only when the cooktop is dry and the storage setup is not trapping moisture against the steel.

Safety: do not use a deeply corroded, structurally damaged, or contaminated cooking surface until it has been properly restored or replaced. Follow the manufacturer’s instructions before using aggressive abrasives or chemicals.
