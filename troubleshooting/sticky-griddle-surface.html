Troubleshooting / Seasoning

Why is my griddle sticky?

A tacky cooktop usually means something is sitting on the surface that never finished becoming seasoning. Most often, that something is too much oil.

Most likely causes
Common

Too much oil

A heavy coating can heat unevenly and leave gummy or tacky patches instead of a hard bonded layer.

Common

Incomplete heating

The oil may not have been heated long enough to finish polymerizing.

Possible

Food or sugar residue

Sticky sauces, marinades, and grease can mimic a seasoning problem.

Possible

Built-up layers

Repeated heavy oiling without proper cleaning can create uneven buildup.

Start with the least aggressive fix

  1. Warm the surface enough to soften residue.
  2. Scrape and wipe away food or grease buildup using methods approved for the cooktop.
  3. If the surface is still tacky from oil, heat it and allow the existing thin residue to finish curing.
  4. After cooling, apply only a whisper-thin film of oil if the surface needs protection.
  5. If thick, flaking, gummy buildup remains, move to a more involved reseasoning process rather than stacking more oil on top.

Do not automatically strip the whole griddle

If most of the cooktop is sound, full stripping may create more work than the original problem. Match the repair to the severity of the buildup.

Safety: use adequate ventilation and follow the appliance manufacturer’s operating instructions. Do not use solvents, oven cleaners, or aggressive chemicals on the cooking surface unless the manufacturer specifically approves them.
