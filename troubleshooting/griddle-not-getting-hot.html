Troubleshooting / Heat

Why is my griddle not getting hot?

Low heat can come from simple operating conditions or from a fuel, regulator, burner, or appliance problem. Start with the boring checks first. Annoyingly, boring checks solve a lot of equipment problems.

Check these first
  1. Give it enough preheat time. A large steel cooktop takes time to absorb heat.
  2. Check burner settings. Confirm the controls are actually set where you think they are.
  3. Consider wind and weather. Strong airflow can pull heat away from the cooktop and disrupt burner performance.
  4. Check the fuel supply. Make sure the propane tank is not empty or nearly empty and that connections are correct.
  5. Compare burner behavior. If one burner is weak while others are normal, the issue may be localized rather than surface-wide.
  6. Consult the manufacturer troubleshooting steps. Regulator reset procedures and approved checks vary by appliance.

Do not bypass gas safety systems

If the appliance has a regulator or safety device that may have tripped, use only the reset procedure specified by the manufacturer. Do not drill or modify burner parts, defeat regulators, or improvise around gas controls.

When the surface seems uneven

Some temperature variation is normal because burner locations, wind, cooktop geometry, and food load affect the steel differently. Preheat the surface, then compare zones rather than expecting every square inch to read identically.

Stop using the appliance if you smell gas, see damaged hoses or fittings, hear an unexplained leak, experience repeated ignition problems, or suspect a regulator or burner fault. Shut off the fuel supply if safe to do so and follow the manufacturer’s service instructions or contact a qualified technician.
