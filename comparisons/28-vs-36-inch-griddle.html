28-inch vs 36-inch griddle

Surface width matters, but it is only half the decision. A griddle's burner count and independently controlled heat zones can change how useful that space is during breakfast, hibachi, burgers, steak, or any cook where several foods need different temperatures.

Factor28-inch class36-inch class
Best fitSmall to medium households, ordinary family mealsLarger households, entertaining, multi-component cooks
FootprintSmaller and easier to placeLarger patio and storage commitment
BatchingMore likely on large breakfasts or crowdsMore room to cook several items at once

PortabilityGenerally easier to moveUsually more of a stay-put appliance
Fuel / operating demandTypically lower overall appliance scaleUsually more total cooking surface and often more burners or elements

Exact dimensions, burner count, heat output, zone layout, and cooking area vary by brand and model. Width alone does not tell you how many temperatures the griddle can manage at once.

Burner count can matter as much as width

A two-burner griddle can usually create a straightforward hot-side / cooler-side workflow. Three independently controlled zones make it easier to run a sear area, a normal cooking area, and a lower finishing or holding area. Four controls give even more flexibility for large breakfasts, hibachi-style meals, or several proteins and sides at once.

That does not mean a four-burner griddle creates four perfectly isolated temperatures. The plate spreads heat across the steel, and food load, wind, preheat time, and burner geometry all affect the surface. The benefit is more control over where heat enters the plate.

Where 17–22 inch griddles fit

Compact 17–22 inch units belong in the same buying conversation even though this page focuses on 28- and 36-inch classes. They make sense for one to three people, camping, tailgating, apartments, small patios, and cooks who value portability more than running several temperature zones at once. Some compact electrics offer more than one independently controlled zone, but the physical surface is still smaller, so zone count and usable workspace must be considered together.

Choose 28-inch class if...

  • You usually cook for about two to five people.
  • You are comfortable with an occasional second batch.
  • Patio or storage space matters.
  • You want enough room for burgers, breakfast, fajitas, and ordinary family meals without buying the largest platform available.
  • You can find a burner or element layout that gives you enough independent heat control for the meals you cook.

Choose 36-inch class if...

  • You regularly cook for five or more people.
  • You host gatherings or cook large breakfasts.
  • You like running several heat zones at the same time.
  • You would rather use more surface area than rotate food through multiple batches.
  • You routinely cook complex meals where three or four independently controlled zones would be useful.

The hidden question is workflow

A larger griddle is not automatically better. Extra surface only helps if your cooking style uses it. A compact cook who mostly makes burgers for four may gain little from extra steel, while somebody doing eggs, bacon, hash browns, pancakes, and sausage simultaneously can use both the extra width and the extra burner controls.

Buying tip: compare size + burner or element count + actual control layout together. A wide griddle with fewer independent controls may be less flexible than a similar-size model with more zones.
