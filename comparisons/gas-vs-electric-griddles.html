Gas vs electric flat-top griddles

Gas is still the traditional choice for large outdoor griddles, but electric has grown far beyond the little breakfast plate on grandma's counter. The useful comparison is not “fire good, outlet bad.” It is where you cook, how much surface you need, what kind of heat control you want, and what maintenance you are willing to live with.

Quick answer

Choose gas if you want a large outdoor cooking surface, strong zone control across a full-size griddle, freedom from electrical outlets, and the traditional seasoned-steel flat-top experience. Choose electric if you value precise thermostat-style control, simpler countertop cooking, easier cleanup on coated surfaces, or an appliance specifically designed for indoor use. There are also serious built-in electric flat tops now, so “electric” no longer automatically means “small.”

Electric is really two categories

One reason electric griddles get compared badly is that people throw every electric appliance into one bucket. A 22-inch dual-zone Blackstone E-Series and a slim breakfast griddle are both electric, but they are not trying to do the same job.

Full-featured electric

Electric flat tops

These behave more like traditional griddles: larger cooking surfaces, multiple zones on some models, digital control, and enough room for full meals.

Countertop

Indoor electric griddles

These prioritize convenience, compact size, easy storage, and breakfast or family-meal duty rather than replacing a patio-sized four-burner machine.

The cleanest comparison may be Blackstone gas vs Blackstone electric

Brand-to-brand debates can get messy because surface material, burner design, size, hood design, and price all change at once. Blackstone gives us a cleaner comparison path because it sells both propane and E-Series electric griddles. Comparing similar-size products within the same brand lets fuel type and surface design become a larger part of the actual question.

Future Flat Top Forge pages should compare pairs such as a 17-inch propane Blackstone versus the 17-inch E-Series, and a 22-inch propane Blackstone versus the 22-inch E-Series, using current manufacturer specifications for both machines.

Electric can change your tool kit too

Traditional gas flat tops commonly use seasoned steel and tolerate the stiff metal spatulas, scrapers, and presses people associate with griddle cooking. Many electric countertop models use ceramic-coated or other nonstick-style surfaces. Blackstone specifically warns against metal utensils on its E-Series ceramic-coated cooktops.

That means moving from gas to electric can change more than the power source. It may also change the spatulas, scraper style, cleaning method, and surface-care habits that make sense.

Gas usually wins when...

  • You want a large outdoor cooking platform.
  • You regularly cook for a crowd.
  • You want multiple burner zones across a broad steel surface.
  • You cook away from convenient electrical outlets.
  • You like the traditional seasoned-steel griddle workflow.
  • You are willing to manage propane, grease, seasoning, and weather protection.

Electric usually wins when...

  • You need an appliance specifically approved for indoor or countertop use.
  • You value thermostat-style or digital temperature control.
  • You cook for a smaller household most of the time.
  • You want less fuel logistics and no propane tank.
  • You prefer a coated cooking surface and simpler wipe-down cleanup.
  • Your patio, balcony, apartment, or storage situation makes a full-size gas cart impractical.

Before buying electric, check the boring electrical stuff

Wattage matters because the griddle has to turn household electrical power into cooking heat. Confirm the appliance voltage, wattage, plug type, circuit requirements, extension-cord restrictions, and indoor/outdoor rating in the manual. A powerful countertop appliance sharing a circuit with half the kitchen is a poor time to discover what a breaker does.

Before buying gas, check fuel and placement

Confirm the required fuel type, regulator setup, outdoor-clearance requirements, storage footprint, hood clearance, and whether natural-gas conversion is officially supported. Do not improvise regulator, orifice, or gas-line modifications outside manufacturer instructions.
