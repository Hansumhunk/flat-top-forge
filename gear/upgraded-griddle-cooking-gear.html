Upgraded griddle cooking gear

The basic kit gets food cooked. The upgraded kit makes larger cooks faster, cleaner, and easier to control by adding redundancy, organization, and a proper workstation.

The five-bottle station

A useful upgraded setup is one bottle for high-heat neutral oil, one for a secondary oil or finishing oil, one for clean water where manufacturer guidance allows it, and two for frequently used sauces or cooking liquids. The exact contents should match what you cook most often. Label everything and refrigerate any sauce, butter mixture, or ingredient that requires refrigeration when it is not actively being used.

Redundancy is the upgrade

The biggest difference between a basic kit and an upgraded station is not novelty. It is having enough of the right tools that one dirty spatula, one occupied dome, or one missing prep tray does not stall the entire cook. Humans eventually discovered this principle in professional kitchens after several thousand years of dropping things.

Use separate prep zones

Keep raw proteins physically separate from cooked food and ready-to-eat ingredients. A practical three-tray system is: raw proteins, prepared vegetables/breads/toppings, and finished food. Add more trays when the meal gets more complex.

What still does not belong here

Specialty taco holders, egg rings, pancake dispensers, fish turners, steaming racks, and similar tools can be useful, but they belong in the specialty layer unless you cook those foods constantly.
