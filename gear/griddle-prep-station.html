How to set up a griddle prep station

The cook gets easier when every tool and ingredient has a job, a location, and a path from raw to cooked. The alternative is waving a spatula around while asking where somebody put the cheese.

Core station zones
ZoneWhat belongs therePurpose
Raw-food zoneRaw meat trays, raw-food utensils, marinadesKeeps uncooked proteins separate from ready-to-eat food.
Ready-to-cook zoneVegetables, buns, tortillas, toppings, pre-portioned ingredientsMakes ingredients easy to reach in cooking order.
Tool zoneSpatulas, scraper, tongs, press, dome, thermometersKeeps hot-work tools in one predictable location.
Liquid / seasoning zoneLabeled squeeze bottles and seasoning shakersLets you oil, season, and sauce without leaving the griddle.
Finished-food zoneClean trays, plates, serving pansProvides a clean destination for cooked food.
Cleanup zoneScraper, paper towels, grease-management supplies, approved cleaning toolsMakes cleanup part of the cook instead of a future archaeological project.

Use at least three trays for a real meal

A practical minimum is one tray for raw protein, one tray for prepared vegetables or other ready-to-cook ingredients, and one clean tray for finished food. More complex meals benefit from more trays, especially when raw poultry, raw beef, breads, toppings, and cooked food are all sharing the same workstation.

Arrange ingredients in cooking order

Place the ingredients you need first closest to the griddle. Put finishing sauces, cheese, buns, and toppings farther back until their turn arrives. The station should follow the sequence of the meal so you are not repeatedly reaching across raw food or hot tools.

Keep a dedicated tool landing area

A metal tray, utensil rest, or designated washable surface gives hot greasy tools somewhere to go between uses. This sounds boring because it is boring. It is also much better than placing a bacon-coated spatula on every available horizontal surface like a tiny stainless-steel crime scene.

Build cleanup into the station

Keep scraping and grease-management supplies close enough that you can clear a zone between stages when needed. Follow the griddle manufacturer's instructions for cleaning methods, water use, and surface care.
