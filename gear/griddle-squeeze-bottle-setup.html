Gear / Bottle Setup

Griddle squeeze bottle setup

Squeeze bottles turn full-size containers into a fast cooking station. The trick is deciding what deserves a bottle, labeling it clearly, and not letting perishable sauces become patio science experiments.

Starter: three bottles
BottleTypical useWhy
1. High-heat neutral oilGeneral griddle cookingLets you apply a controlled thin film without pouring directly from a large container.
2. Clean waterLimited steaming, loosening residue, or cleanup only where manufacturer guidance allowsUseful in small controlled amounts instead of carrying a pitcher around the cooktop.
3. Frequent sauceSoy sauce, teriyaki, house sauce, or another liquid you use oftenSpeeds repeat cooks and gives better control over application.
Upgraded: five bottles
BottleSuggested roleExamples
1Primary high-heat oilNeutral cooking oil suited to your heat range
2Secondary oil / finishing oilOlive oil or another oil you intentionally use for a different flavor or job
3WaterFor manufacturer-approved steaming or cleanup use
4Primary savory sauceSoy sauce, teriyaki, hibachi-style sauce
5Recipe-specific liquidBBQ sauce, hot sauce blend, thin butter mixture, or another frequently used sauce

The five-bottle setup is a workflow template, not a commandment. If you cook smash burgers every weekend and hibachi twice a year, your bottles should reflect that.

Label everything

Use waterproof labels or another durable system that identifies every bottle at a glance. Similar-looking oil, water, vinegar, and sauce bottles become much less funny once one of them reaches a hot cooktop unexpectedly.

Perishable ingredients do not get patio citizenship

Sauces, butter mixtures, dairy-based liquids, or other ingredients that require refrigeration should be refrigerated when they are not actively being used. Clean bottles between refills rather than endlessly topping old product with new product.

Choose bottle size by workflow

Small bottles are easier to label, clean, and swap for occasional sauces. Larger bottles make more sense for high-use oil or water during long cooks. A matching set looks tidy, but function matters more than turning the prep cart into a catalog photograph.
