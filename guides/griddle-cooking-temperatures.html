Flat-top griddle cooking temperatures

You do not need one magic number for every food. You need a useful temperature range, a properly preheated surface, and the ability to move food when the steel starts winning the argument.

Practical starting ranges
FoodSurface rangeBest use
EggsLow to mediumGentle cooking with less sticking or browning
Pancakes / French toastMediumEven browning without burning the outside
VegetablesMedium to medium-highColor plus enough time to soften
Chicken piecesMedium to medium-highBrown outside while cooking through safely
Smash burgersHighFast crust formation and crisp edges
SteakHigh for sear, lower to finish if neededBuild browning first, then manage doneness

Exact surface temperatures vary by griddle, weather, food load, burner layout, and measuring method. Use internal food temperatures and safe-cooking guidance for doneness, especially with poultry and ground meat.

Surface temperature is only half the story

A griddle can read hot when empty and then drop sharply when covered with cold food. Give ingredients room, cook in batches when needed, and avoid burying the entire cooktop under one refrigerator-cold pile unless steaming everything was secretly the plan.

Use zones instead of chasing one number

A hot zone lets you brown food quickly while a medium or lower zone gives you somewhere to finish, hold, or recover from an overenthusiastic sear.

Food-safety note: surface temperature does not prove that food is safely cooked. Use a food thermometer and current food-safety guidance for internal doneness.
