How to clean a flat-top griddle

The goal is not to make the cooktop look factory-new after every meal. It is to remove food residue, protect the seasoning, and leave the surface ready for the next cook.

After-cook routine
  1. Turn the heat down or off. Work while the surface is still warm enough to loosen residue, but not dangerously hot.
  2. Scrape toward the grease channel. Use a griddle-safe scraper and avoid gouging the surface.
  3. Loosen stubborn residue carefully. Follow the manufacturer’s cleaning method for your model. Some allow small amounts of water on a warm surface; others may specify different care.
  4. Wipe the surface clean. Remove loosened food, grease, and moisture.
  5. Dry completely. Bare or lightly seasoned steel should not be left wet.
  6. Leave a very light protective film if appropriate. A thin wipe of suitable oil can help protect carbon-steel or rolled-steel cooktops between uses.

What not to do automatically

Do not reach for oven cleaner, aggressive chemicals, power tools, or heavy abrasives just because the surface looks dark or uneven. A seasoned cooktop is supposed to develop color. Use aggressive restoration methods only when the condition actually calls for them and when the manufacturer permits them.

Grease management matters too

Empty grease cups before they overflow, remove trapped food, and keep the grease path clear. A clean cooktop paired with a forgotten grease tray is a strangely efficient way to create tomorrow’s problem.

Before storage

Make sure the appliance is cool, dry, and protected from weather according to the manufacturer’s instructions. If the griddle lives outdoors, a well-fitting cover and dry storage conditions matter more than heroic restoration work every few months.
