How to season a flat-top griddle

The basic system is simple: clean steel, very thin oil, enough heat to polymerize it, and repetition. The part humans keep trying to improve is the “very thin” part.

Quick answer

For a new or freshly stripped steel cooktop, clean and dry the surface, apply a very thin coat of a suitable cooking oil, heat the griddle until the oil finishes smoking off, let the surface cool slightly, and repeat for several thin layers. Follow the manufacturer’s instructions for your specific model when they differ.

What seasoning actually does

Seasoning is not a puddle of oil left on the surface. Heat changes a thin film of oil into a bonded layer that helps protect the steel and improves release over time. A good surface is built through repeated thin layers and regular cooking.

Step-by-step

  1. Start clean. Remove shipping residue, loose debris, or old flaking material as appropriate for the condition of the cooktop.
  2. Dry the steel completely. Moisture left on bare steel invites rust.
  3. Preheat the griddle. Bring the surface up gradually according to the appliance instructions.
  4. Add a small amount of oil. Spread it across the cooking surface, edges, and corners where appropriate.
  5. Wipe it thinner than feels necessary. The steel should look lightly coated, not wet.
  6. Heat until the oil finishes smoking off. Keep the area well ventilated and follow the appliance’s outdoor-use requirements.
  7. Repeat. Several light cycles generally build a better base than one heavy application.
  8. Finish with routine cooking and care. The surface typically improves as it is used and maintained.

What oil should you use?

Use a cooking oil the griddle manufacturer considers suitable for seasoning. Common choices include canola, vegetable, avocado, and other high-heat cooking oils. The exact oil matters less than using a very thin layer and heating it correctly.

Signs you used too much oil

If the surface stays tacky, gummy, or patchy after cooling, the coating may have been too heavy or incompletely heated. Do not automatically strip the whole griddle. Start with the least aggressive correction that matches the problem.
