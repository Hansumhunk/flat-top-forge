Tips & Tricks / Smash Burgers

How to get crispy smash-burger edges

The crust comes from contact, heat, and timing. Repeatedly flattening a burger after it has started cooking mostly squeezes out juice and announces distrust in physics.

The trick in one sentence

Start with a loose ball of beef, put it on a very hot dry-enough surface, smash once immediately and firmly, then leave it alone until the edges turn deeply browned and lacy.

Step by step

  1. Preheat a high-heat zone thoroughly.
  2. Use loosely formed beef balls instead of tightly packed patties.
  3. Place the beef down and smash within the first several seconds, while the meat is still cold and unseared.
  4. Press the outer edge thin so more meat contacts the steel.
  5. Season after the smash, then stop pressing.
  6. Wait for deep browning around the edge before scraping underneath with a stiff metal spatula.
  7. Flip once, add cheese if using, and finish quickly.

What usually ruins the crust

  • Surface not hot enough
  • Too much meat per ball
  • Overcrowding the griddle
  • Moving the patty too early
  • Using a flexible spatula that cannot scrape the crust cleanly
