How to make diner-crispy hash browns

The main enemies are moisture, crowding, and human impatience. Potatoes do not need encouragement every twelve seconds.

The five-part trick

  1. Thaw frozen shredded potatoes completely or shred fresh potatoes.
  2. Squeeze out as much moisture as practical with a clean towel.
  3. Heat a medium-high zone and add a thin but complete layer of oil or other suitable cooking fat.
  4. Spread the potatoes in a thin, even layer and season lightly.
  5. Leave them alone until a real crust forms before flipping in sections.

Thin beats thick

A thick mound traps steam inside itself. A thinner layer gives more potato direct contact with hot steel, which means more browning and less soft, damp center.

Flip sections, not confetti

Once the underside is deeply browned, divide the hash browns into manageable sections and turn them with a wide spatula. Constant stirring prevents the crust from ever becoming one coherent thing.
