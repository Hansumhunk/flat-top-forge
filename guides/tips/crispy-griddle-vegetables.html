How to char vegetables instead of steaming them

Browning needs heat and space. Piling wet vegetables into one crowded mound is how you accidentally open a tiny vegetable sauna.

The core rules

  1. Dry washed vegetables before they hit the griddle.
  2. Preheat a medium-high or high zone appropriate for the vegetable.
  3. Use enough oil for surface contact without drowning the food.
  4. Spread vegetables in a single layer with room between pieces.
  5. Let one side brown before stirring or flipping.
  6. Combine with sauces, butter, citrus, or finishing seasonings near the end.

Cut size controls the result

Thin pieces brown quickly but can overcook fast. Larger pieces need more time and may benefit from a medium zone after the first char. Keep pieces reasonably consistent so half the batch is not done while the other half is still negotiating with rawness.

Salt with intention

Salt can draw moisture from vegetables. For high-moisture vegetables, seasoning lightly just before or during cooking can make it easier to preserve browning than salting heavily long before they hit the griddle.
