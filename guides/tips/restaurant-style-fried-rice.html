How to make better griddle fried rice

The goal is fried rice, not warm rice that has been politely introduced to soy sauce. Dry grains, enough heat, and proper sequencing make the difference.

Start with cold rice

Chilled cooked rice is firmer and less steamy than freshly cooked rice. Break apart any large clumps before it reaches the griddle so the grains can contact the steel instead of traveling as one refrigerated brick.

Use separate jobs and zones

  1. Preheat a medium-high to high cooking zone and keep a lower holding zone available.
  2. Cook egg separately and move it aside while still tender.
  3. Cook vegetables or protein separately so they do not dump moisture into the rice.
  4. Add a thin layer of rice to the hotter zone and give it time to brown before tossing.
  5. Add butter or oil in controlled amounts as needed.
  6. Add soy sauce and other liquid seasonings late, then toss quickly so the rice absorbs flavor without bathing in liquid.
  7. Fold the egg, vegetables, and protein back in at the end.

Do not overload the surface

A massive pile traps steam. If the rice looks wet instead of sizzling and separating, reduce the batch size or spread it across more hot surface.
