Tips & Tricks / Steak

Use a sear zone and a butter zone for steak

High heat is excellent for crust. It is less excellent for minced garlic and butter, which can go from aromatic to archaeological evidence with impressive speed.

Build two jobs into the griddle

Use one hot zone for browning and a second medium or lower zone for finishing. The steak earns its crust first, then moves somewhere calmer before butter, garlic, or herbs enter the conversation.

Step by step

  1. Pat the steak dry and season it.
  2. Preheat a hot sear zone and a lower finishing zone.
  3. Sear the steak without moving it excessively until the first side develops strong browning.
  4. Flip and sear the second side.
  5. Move the steak to the lower zone before adding butter, garlic, and herbs.
  6. Turn or baste briefly as the steak approaches your preferred internal doneness.
  7. Remove and rest before slicing.

Use temperature, not guesswork

An instant-read thermometer is the useful tool for internal doneness. The infrared thermometer can help understand the cooking surface, but it cannot tell you whether the center of the steak is where you want it.
